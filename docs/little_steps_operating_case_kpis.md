# Little Steps Nursery operating case and KPIs

> This is a pseudonymised operating case. It is not audited client financial information.

Little Steps Nursery represents a credible early-childhood microenterprise with no dedicated digital-transformation team. Cleo is its owner or director persona and the economic buyer for a service such as Kinder Signs. Every number below carries an evidence label.

## Cleo and the operating problem

Cleo directs administration, supports Baby Sign activity, teaches or supports courses, answers family questions, checks quality, supports school relationships and handles commercial work. Her management time is limited. The founder-observed problem is that Baby Sign knowledge can earn one-off course income but does not automatically become a structured recurring school-family service.

## Tuition envelope

Founder-observed inputs are a 09:00-17:30 full schedule, an approximate full fee of EUR 600 per month, approximately 80% leaving around 16:00 and approximately 20% using the full schedule.

Calculated shorter-day fee:

```text
EUR 600 x 7 hours / 8.5 hours = approximately EUR 494
```

Calculated weighted fee:

```text
80% x EUR 494 + 20% x EUR 600 = approximately EUR 515 per child per month
```

## Current Baby Sign and communication context

These are founder-observed workflows in one nursery context, not universal claims about a vendor or the sector:

- Baby Sign is already used in the nursery, and classroom staff know or use it.
- Cleo sells Baby Sign courses to parents and other schools.
- Parents ask for sign lists or supporting material.
- Monetisation is mainly one-off course or training based.
- The knowledge is not consistently converted into a recurring, structured school-family content service.
- Little Steps uses Pequebook for school-family communication.
- In this observed context, communication can include broad circulars, menu details inside general updates, limited family-level granularity, group-based photos, delayed chat responses and repeated family questions.

## Commercial implications

The proposed core model is a per-centre school subscription with optional future content add-ons. It aligns the payer with school-level value, avoids making an individual child profile the billing unit and supports central reuse.
