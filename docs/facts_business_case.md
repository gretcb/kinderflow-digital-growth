# Business-case facts

These are inputs for later analysis, not an ROI conclusion.

## KNOWN

| Fact | Repository evidence |
|---|---|
| KinderFlow is the platform and Kinder Signs is the first selected use case. | `use_case_definition.md`; `feedback/round1_decision.md` |
| The problem being tested is continuity between a sign used at school and matching guidance at home. | `use_case_definition.md` |
| The primary customer/payer hypothesis is the nursery school or school group. | `use_case_definition.md`; `feedback/round1_decision.md` |
| Educators and families are users; the child aged 0–3 is the intended beneficiary. | `use_case_definition.md` |
| The model is school-led B2B / B2B2C. | `use_case_definition.md`; `feedback/round1_decision.md` |
| Kinder Signs prepares the master content. Schools select and assign available items. | `feedback/round1_decision.md`; prototype role separation |
| The prototype represents flashcards, stories and songs as potential service/content access. No billing exists. | `prototype/admin.html`; `prototype/school.html`; `prototype/content-studio.html` |
| Round 1 estimated an 8–9 week validation period. | `cost_timeline/estimate.md` |
| Round 1 estimated a validation budget of €5.5k–€17.8k. | `cost_timeline/estimate.md` |
| That estimate is a validation budget, not a launch budget. | `cost_timeline/estimate.md` |
| The largest listed cost drivers are expert review, user research, original content production, educator testing and multi-sign technical refinement. | `cost_timeline/estimate.md` |
| No real pilot results exist in the repository. | Evidence review; pilot files define future events only |

## ASSUMPTION

| Assumption | Why it is only an assumption |
|---|---|
| Schools will pay for school-home continuity. | Round 1 supports further validation, not a purchase decision. |
| A centrally produced sign can be reused across entitled schools at acceptable marginal cost. | The architecture supports reuse; operating costs and demand are not measured. |
| Premium flashcard/story access can increase value. | The prototype shows entitlements, but no package has been sold or tested. |
| Educators can assign material with low effort. | The static flow is simple; task time in a real school is unknown. |
| Families will use matching guidance more than disconnected free resources. | This is the core desirability hypothesis; no behavioural pilot data exists. |
| Professional review can be organised at a sustainable cost and speed. | Review is a required control, but the operating model is not tested. |

## TO VALIDATE

- School willingness to pay and who owns the budget.
- Pricing and packaging; no price has been selected.
- Whether families pay for any extension or whether the school pays for all access.
- School acquisition and support costs.
- Professional review cost, turnaround and rework rate.
- Content-production time per sign after the first five.
- Educator activation and assignment completion.
- Family viewing, printing and repeat use.
- Retention and expansion after an initial pilot.
- Pilot economics and any credible return calculation.

Do not turn the €5.5k–€17.8k validation estimate into a product price or ROI result.
